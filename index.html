The croissant is a light, flaky, and buttery bread that originated in France and is now enjoyed worldwide as a popular pastry. Shaped like a crescent moon, the croissant is made from layers of yeast-leavened dough and butter, which are repeatedly folded and rolled to create its signature airy texture. When baked, these thin layers puff up beautifully, giving the croissant its crispy golden-brown crust and soft, delicate interior. It is often eaten plain, but many people enjoy it filled with chocolate, cheese, ham, or even cream for extra flavor. Croissants are usually served warm, making them perfect for breakfast or as a snack with coffee or tea. Their rich buttery taste and elegant appearance have made them a symbol of French baking and a favorite treat around the world.
